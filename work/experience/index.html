stop 3 · accepted at BMVC 2026

Why Multi-Source Audio Fails to Compose

Anurag Singh, Prajwal Singh, Shanmuganathan Raman. Why Multi-Source Audio Fails to Compose: A Geometric Diagnosis and Its Remedy. Accepted at the British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC), 2026.

1 · the setup

A dog barks. A guitar plays.

Give an audio-to-image model both sounds and ask for a picture. You'd expect a dog and a guitar.

2 · the failure

You get a dog-guitar

Models either forget one sound entirely (neglect) or melt both into one strange object (fusion). Adding more sources makes it worse.

3 · the diagnosis

The sounds are squashed together

We looked at where the audio tokens land in CLIP space. Tokens from different sounds sit displaced and crowded together. We measured this with clustering: intra-class compactness, angular alignment and inter-token separation. The failure is geometric.

4 · the remedy

Push them apart, on purpose

At inference time we mix Attend-and-Excite, which makes sure every sound gets attention, with an A* spatial loss, which keeps their attention maps in different places. We also rebuilt the embedder around a CLAP audio encoder, with one token per audio source (multi-token extension).

5 · the result

Both of them show up

We built benchmarks from VGG-Sound with 1 to 5 audio sources (VGG2, VGG3, VGGn) and extended six audio-to-image metrics to the multi-source setting. The method ranks best on four of six metrics across five benchmarks.

industry · may – jul 2026 · Skan AI

Data Science Intern, Process Intelligence

Process intelligence means learning how work really happens from interaction logs: clicks, forms, windows. Those logs are noisy, and the noise turns clean processes into spaghetti.

  • Built a rule-based noise framework using field- and feature-level heuristics. Every flagged event gets a readable reason.
  • Designed an event reordering module that groups fragmented, interleaved traces back into complete process instances.
  • Explored VLM-based key–value extraction with prompt normalisation.
  • Result: a better signal-to-noise ratio, consistently fewer process variants, and simpler process graphs.
