Cycle Sound: Bidirectional Audio–Image Generation

PyTorchdVAETransformersVGG-Sound

The idea

Sound and pictures speak different languages, so Cycle Sound gives each one a small vocabulary. A discrete VAE turns an image into a grid of codebook tokens, and a sound's Mel-spectrogram becomes a sequence of audio tokens.

Translating between them is then a sequence-to-sequence problem. A transformer decoder reads the tokens from one side and writes the tokens for the other, and the matching decoder turns them back into pixels or sound. The same recipe runs in both directions, which is the loop in the drawing.

My contributions

  • Implemented ViT, CLIP, a VAE and DALL·E v1 as the building blocks for cross-modal generation.
  • Built the audio → image pipeline: Mel-spectrogram → audio tokens → transformer decoder → image tokens → image dVAE decoder.
  • Built the image → audio pipeline: image tokens → transformer decoder → audio dVAE → spectrogram → waveform via an inverse Mel transform.
  • Trained discrete VAEs with Gumbel-Softmax quantisation, then the transformer decoders, in two stages on 24,000+ image–audio pairs from VGG-Sound.
  • Wrote the VGG-Sound download and extraction scripts, and published a project website.

Adversarial Robustness of Vision Transformers

PyTorchViTCIFAR-10adversarial attacks

The idea

A Vision Transformer cuts an image into patches, and each patch carries a note saying where it came from: the positional encoding. Normally that note is added onto the patch, so position and content are blended into the same numbers. That's the top strip in the drawing.

We asked whether a different kind of note makes ViTs harder to fool. Our encoding concatenates patch and positional embeddings in a fixed ratio (bottom strip), keeping position in its own slot, and it held up better under attack.

My contributions

  • Evaluated the adversarial robustness of ViTs with sinusoidal, learnable and RoPE positional embeddings.
  • Proposed a novel positional encoding that concatenates positional and patch embeddings in a fixed ratio, and swept ratios such as 3:1 and 4:1.
  • Benchmarked 400+ ViT architectures on CIFAR-10 under black-box frequency-domain (phase and magnitude) attacks.
  • Achieved a 2% accuracy improvement, outperforming the standard positional encodings across attacks.

DAGMM for Anomaly Detection

PyTorchVAEVQ-VAEGMMKDDCUP

The idea

DAGMM (Deep Autoencoding Gaussian Mixture Model) finds anomalies in two moves. An autoencoder squeezes every sample into a tiny summary. Then a Gaussian mixture learns where the summaries of normal data usually land.

Anything that lands far from every cluster gets a high energy score and is flagged, like the two sheep circled in the drawing. We took the model apart one piece at a time to see which changes actually help.

My contributions

  • Evaluated DAGMM on KDDCUP under architectural, loss-function, inference-criterion and mixture-model modifications.
  • Replaced the autoencoder with a VAE and a VQ-VAE, gaining 1.83% and 2.24% in F1-score.
  • Designed a refined inference criterion combining reconstruction loss with sample energy, improving recall by 0.19% at comparable F1.
  • Tested a Laplacian mixture model, a supervised energy penalty in the loss, and a semi-supervised threshold setup.
