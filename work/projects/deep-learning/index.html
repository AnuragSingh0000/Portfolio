Cycle Sound

Pick a direction and watch the tokens travel.

Most models go one way. Cycle Sound goes both: hear a dog bark and draw a dog, or see a dog and make it bark.

Both directions share one idea. Squeeze the sound or image into a short sequence of discrete tokens with a VQ-VAE, then let a transformer translate one token language into the other.

under the hood
  • Implemented ViT, CLIP, a VAE and DALL·E v1 as building blocks
  • VQ-VAE + transformer pipelines for audio → image and image → audio
  • 24,000+ image–audio pairs from VGG-Sound, trained in two stages

Can you trick a Vision Transformer?

A ViT reads an image like a jigsaw: it cuts it into patches, and each patch needs a name tag saying where it came from. That tag is the positional encoding.

The question: does the kind of name tag change how easily an attacker can fool the model? I compared sinusoidal, learnable and RoPE tags. Then I proposed a new one: instead of adding the tag onto the patch, staple it alongside (concatenate, in a fixed ratio), so position information stays in its own slot instead of being mixed into the patch.

under the hood
  • Benchmarked 400+ ViT architectures on CIFAR-10 under black-box attacks
  • The concatenated encoding gained about 2% accuracy and beat standard positional encodings across attacks

Find the odd sheep

Twenty sheep. Two of them aren't quite sheep. Can you spot them?

DAGMM finds anomalies in two moves. An autoencoder squashes each sample into a tiny summary, and a Gaussian mixture learns where "normal" summaries usually land. Anything far from every cluster is suspicious.

I took DAGMM apart and rebuilt pieces of it: new architectures, losses, inference criteria and mixture models.

  • Swap the autoencoder for a VAE: +1.83% F1
  • Swap it for a VQ-VAE: +2.24% F1
  • A refined inference criterion: +0.19% recall at comparable F1
