A simplified tree for show. The real one split on TSFEL features from phone accelerometer and gyroscope signals.

A phone in your pocket records tiny wobbles. From those alone, can a model tell whether you're walking, climbing stairs, sitting or lying down?

I extracted features with TSFEL, trained decision trees, and visualised the classes with PCA. Then I wrote a decision tree from scratch and raced it against scikit-learn's.

Finish Tolstoy's sentence

A toy word-pair model built from the opening lines of War and Peace (Maude translation), so it runs in your browser. The real project was a neural network with learned embeddings, trained on the whole book.

No labels, just one very long book. The model reads War and Peace, learns a vector for every word, and guesses what comes next. Tolstoy supplies his own answer key.

I built the network myself, with adjustable hyperparameters, so the same book can be read with different context sizes and embedding widths.

research reproductionSchNet · sGDML

Teaching a network physics

Ethanol, a classic molecular dynamics benchmark. The toy springs here stand in for the learned energy surface.

Simulating molecules with quantum chemistry is painfully slow. SchNet and sGDML learn the energy surface from examples instead. Forces fall out as its slope, so a simulation can take millions of cheap steps.

I reproduced and extended published molecular dynamics experiments with both model families.

under the hood
  • Dataset conversion scripts (XYZ → NPZ) and database preparation for both pipelines
  • Training and evaluation setups for force-field and energy-surface prediction
