Ultra-Fast Packet Crafter

Packets are paper planes. Normally every plane stops at the kernel's post office: it gets copied, stamped and queued before it reaches the network card.

I took Blitzping, a packet-flooding tool, and changed it to craft custom UDP packets directly in memory instead of TCP. Then I added a DPDK kernel bypass: the program talks to the NIC directly with zero copies, and the planes skip the post office entirely.

4.5×packet throughput vs. standard Blitzping
6.7×bit-rate transfer

Addresses here are made up. The mechanism is the real one.

A plane with a fuel gauge

The tool also includes a TTL-based traceroute. Each UDP probe carries a time-to-live: a fuel gauge that loses one bar at every router.

When the fuel runs out, that router mails back an ICMP "time exceeded" postcard, and now you know who it is. Send the next probe with one more bar of fuel and it gets one hop further, until the destination answers.
